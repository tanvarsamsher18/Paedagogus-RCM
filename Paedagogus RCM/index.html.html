Oncology billing operates at the intersection of pharmaceutical distribution and clinical service delivery. The Buy-and-Bill model means your practice acquires high-cost drugs and bills the payer — a process with a dozen failure points that most billing teams are not equipped to manage.

  • Infusion hierarchy coding (Initial 96413 → Sequential 96415 → Concurrent 96417) with zero hierarchy inversion
  • JW/JZ modifier application on every single-dose vial — Medicare-compliant, audit-proof
  • ASP+6% drug pricing validation against quarterly CMS ASP tables
  • Buy-and-bill acquisition cost reconciliation vs. reimbursement monitoring
  • Prior authorization for high-cost agents: Pembrolizumab, Nivolumab, Rituximab, Carboplatin combinations

Cardiology RCM

Cardiology practices face a unique billing paradox: high-value procedures with razor-thin error tolerance. A single modifier error on a cardiac catheterization or a bundling issue on an echocardiogram can trigger a cascade of denials across an entire payer contract.

  • Cardiac catheterization coding — right heart, left heart, combined procedures (93451–93461) with proper add-on code sequencing
  • Echocardiography complete vs. limited (93306/93307) with Doppler and color flow documentation requirements
  • Stress testing hierarchy — exercise, pharmacological, nuclear (93015–93018, 78451–78454)
  • Implantable device billing — ICD, pacemaker, loop recorder — facility vs. professional split billing
  • Cardiology infusion billing: Amiodarone, Dobutamine, Milrinone (high-cost drug protocols)
  • Medicare CPTS payment review — global vs. component billing for interventional procedures
